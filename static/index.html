Digital Rights Projects

Agent-Based Model Observatory · 43 Cases
Welcome

This observatory is a companion to the dissertation Digital Rights Projects: Collective Capacities for Democratizing Technology. It computationally operationalizes findings from 43 transnational cases of community-led digital initiatives, allowing you to explore how design choices, governance structures, and organizational conditions generate sovereignty outcomes.

The model is built for several audiences:

Researchers & Academics
Examine condition-outcome relationships, test theoretical claims through sensitivity analysis, compare organizational configurations, and explore how sovereignty is constructed across case types.
Digital Rights Project Teams
Find cases similar to your initiative, understand which conditions most strongly influence your outcome goals, and use the Case Constructor to model how different design choices might affect your results.
Communities & Practitioners
Explore how community-led initiatives build democratic practices, generate community wealth, balance local and global priorities, and achieve lasting impact. Learn from the strategies of 43 real-world projects.
Policymakers & Funders
Understand which organizational conditions most effectively produce democratic governance, community wealth, and sovereignty outcomes. Use ecosystem-level analysis to inform investment and policy design.
Model Structure & Navigation
▶

The observatory contains two models. M1 (Primary Model) analyzes how organizational conditions produce sovereignty outcomes through production rules and mechanisms. M2 (Relational Model) analyzes the partnership ecosystems and inter-case relationships that shape how initiatives connect to each other and to broader networks. Together, they provide a comprehensive view of how communities construct digital sovereignty.

M1 has seven pages. M2 has one page with several analytical tabs. Click any page name below to navigate directly.

M1: Case Explorer
Browse all 43 cases. Select any case to see its full profile: conditions, outcomes, sovereignty terrain and capacity, partners, production rules, and recursive patterns. The starting point for case-level analysis.
M1: Comparison
Select any two cases and compare them side-by-side across all profile dimensions. Useful for understanding how different organizational configurations produce different outcomes.
M1: Sensitivity Lab
Take any existing case and modify its condition scores to build counterfactual scenarios. The model predicts how outcomes, sovereignty, and mechanisms would change. Tests which conditions matter most for each case.
M1: Case Constructor
Build an entirely new case from scratch by setting condition scores across all 11 condition types. The model predicts outcomes, sovereignty, production rules, and the 5 most similar empirical cases. Useful for modeling proposed initiatives or testing theoretical configurations.
M1: D-A-S Patterns
Explore 17 recursive Design-Affordance-Sovereignty patterns that describe how conditions enable affordances that construct sovereignty. View which cases participate in each pattern, the conditions and outcomes involved, and the theoretical logic.
M1: Analytical Ops
Advanced analytical operations including ecosystem-wide sensitivity matrices, reverse analysis (which conditions produce a given outcome), condition interaction analysis, feature analysis, counter-case generation, and environment profiling.
M1: Ecosystem
Ecosystem-level statistics and distributions across all 43 cases: condition-outcome frequencies, generative logic, organization type comparisons, outcome distributions, sovereignty landscape, D-A-S pattern distributions, and model validation results.
M2: Relational
The second model analyzes relational ecosystems: partnership diversity, relationship types, network reach, inter-case connectivity, latent cluster analysis, agent-agent interactions, matched pair comparisons, and relational sensitivity testing.
Reading the Displays: A Page-by-Page Guide
▶
Case Explorer

Internal Conditions are the design choices an initiative controls: Technology Practices (TP), Resources (RS), Governance (GV), Technology Tools (TT), Partners (PT), and Purpose (PU). External Conditions are contextual factors the initiative navigates: Organizational Information (OI), Politics (PO), Regulation (RG), Social Context (SC), and Problem Recognition (PR).

Condition scores range from -0.25 to +1.00. Each score is the sum of scores assigned to individual condition categories present in the case, capped at the range boundaries. Category scores derive from three empirical inputs: resonance (total connections to outcomes across the 25×56 matrix), necessary condition relationships (100% coverage for specific outcomes), and case count (number of cases exhibiting the category). A score of +1.00 indicates foundational conditions; +0.75 strongly constructive; +0.50 constructive; +0.25 enabling; 0.00 neutral; -0.25 navigational. Tags below each score show which specific categories are present.

Example: Condition score display
Governance 0.750
GV1: Decentralized Authority Distribution GV3: Federated Multi-Scale Coordination
This case scores 0.75 in Governance because it has two governance categories (GV1 at +0.50 and GV3 at +0.25).

Outcomes are organized into four groups: Democratic Practices (DP), Community Value (CV), Balance (BL), and Impact (IM). Outcome scores range from 0.000 to 1.000, calculated as the sum of base × rarity_weight / normalization_factor for each achieved category. Rarity weight = 10 / n_cases, so outcomes achieved by fewer cases contribute more to the score.

Example: Outcome score display
Democratic Practices 0.650
DP1: Decentralized Organizational Governance DP5: Open Access and Transparent Systems
DP1 appears in 10 cases (rarity weight 1.0); DP5 in 5 cases (rarity weight 2.0). Rarer outcomes contribute more.

Sovereignty: Terrain vs. Constructed Capacity maps the case across seven sovereignty dimensions. Terrain reflects which sovereignty dimensions the case's conditions operate on. Constructed Capacity reflects what the case's outcomes actually build. Where capacity exceeds terrain, the case constructs emergent sovereignty beyond its operating context. Where terrain exceeds capacity, there is unrealized potential.

Example: Sovereignty dimension
Economic
0.500 0.750
Capacity (0.750) exceeds terrain (0.500): this case constructs emergent Economic sovereignty beyond where its conditions operate.

Partners & Relational Ecosystem shows inter-case connections (documented direct partnerships with other cases in the dataset), partner types engaged (up to 12 types of organizations the case partners with), relationship types (up to 6 types of relationships maintained), and relational density components (D1–D4 scores).

Production Rules are the generative pathways connecting conditions to outcomes for each case. Each rule shows a condition type producing an outcome group through a specific mechanism. A mechanism describes how the condition produces the outcome.

Example: Production rule
Governance → structural → Democratic Practices
The case's governance conditions produce democratic practice outcomes through a structural mechanism: establishing organizational forms and governance structures that enable participation.

The five mechanism types are: enabling (creating capacities and resources), structural (establishing organizational forms), connective (building relationships and networks), legitimizing (establishing authority and recognition), and protective (defending rights and boundaries).

Condition Interactions describe how the case's conditions work together, including synergies and dependencies. Synthesis provides a narrative summary of the case's configurational logic. Compact Equation is a formalized expression of condition-outcome relationships, displayed in monospace.

Recursive D-A-S Patterns show which of the 17 Design-Affordance-Sovereignty patterns the case participates in. Each pattern represents a recurring configuration where specific design choices create affordances that construct specific sovereignty dimensions. For example, #2 Economic Sovereignty describes how resource and governance conditions create economic affordances.

Sensitivity Lab

Select an existing case and use the sliders to modify its condition scores. Each slider position corresponds to an empirically observed score level, and the category tags below each slider show which condition categories correspond to that score. Toggle "N/A" to remove a condition entirely.

After running the analysis, the Predicted Production Rules are derived from the 5 most similar empirical cases to the modified profile. Rules labeled generative exist in the base case's own production rules; configurational rules are observed in similar cases but not the base case, representing emergent possibilities from the new configuration.

Mechanisms Engaged lists the mechanism types activated in the predicted production rules, each with a weight.

Example: Mechanisms Engaged
enabling (4.5) structural (1.2) connective (0.8)
The weight is the sum of similarity-weighted mechanism scores across the 5 reference cases. A higher number means that mechanism operates more prominently across the most similar cases. Here, enabling mechanisms dominate at 4.5, meaning the reference cases primarily create capacities and resources.

D-A-S Patterns Enacted shows which recursive sovereignty patterns the modified configuration would participate in, each with a weight.

Example: D-A-S pattern weights
#2 Economic Sovereignty 2.10
#5 Social Sovereignty 1.40
The weight reflects similarity-weighted participation across the 5 reference cases. A weight of 2.10 means the pattern is strongly active in multiple highly similar cases. Higher weights indicate more robust predictions.

Outcome Effects compares baseline and predicted outcome scores, showing which categories are gained, retained, or lost. Sovereignty Effects compares terrain and capacity before and after the modification, identifying where potential expands or contracts.

Case Constructor

Build a hypothetical case from scratch. Name the case, optionally assign an organization type, and set each condition score using the sliders. Conditions left as "N/A" are absent from the case, distinct from scoring 0.00 (present but neutral).

The results display a Condition Profile showing the interpretation, mechanism type, and sovereignty terrain for each active condition, along with category tags. The remaining sections (Production Rules, Mechanisms Engaged, D-A-S Patterns, Outcomes, Sovereignty, and Similar Cases) use the same analytical logic as the Sensitivity Lab.

Example: Condition profile entry in Case Constructor
Technology Practices 0.75
Strongly Constructive · enabling · Terrain: Technical Infrastructure
TP1: Democratic Data Authority (n=5) TP2: Open Development and Transparency (n=4)
The "(n=5)" indicates 5 of the 43 cases exhibit this category. The mechanism and terrain show how this condition operates and which sovereignty dimensions it engages.

Prediction Basis shows the 5 most similar cases with a similarity percentage, revealing which empirical cases the model draws its predictions from.

D-A-S Patterns

Each of the 17 patterns is displayed as a card showing: participating cases, the condition types and outcome groups involved, the sovereignty dimensions constructed, a description of the pattern's theoretical logic, and links to member cases. Click any pattern to expand its detail view.

Analytical Operations

This page provides six advanced analytical operations via tabs. Sensitivity Matrix shows ecosystem-wide outcome changes when each condition type is varied by a chosen delta. Reverse Analysis works backwards from a chosen outcome group or sovereignty dimension to identify which conditions most strongly produce it. Condition Interactions tests how pairs of conditions jointly affect outcomes. Feature Analysis examines how organizational features (e.g. maturity, geographic scope) relate to outcomes. Counter-Case Lab generates opposite condition profiles for each case and predicts the results. Environment Profiling simulates external environment changes across the ecosystem.

Ecosystem

Ecosystem-level analysis across all 43 cases. Overview provides aggregate statistics. Condition-Outcome shows frequency matrices. Generative Logic maps causal pathways. Org Types compares the four organizational types. Sovereignty Landscape visualizes terrain and capacity distributions. Validation shows internal consistency tests, cross-validation, and calibration assessments.

M2: Relational Model

The relational model analyzes inter-case networks. Key tabs include Density Analysis (composite scores and rankings), Partner Types (12 categories of organizational partnerships), Cluster Analysis (latent organizational groupings), Agent Interactions (pairwise similarity), and Matched Pairs (cases sharing configurations for systematic comparison).

Glossary of Key Concepts
▶
Conditions → Outcomes
Conditions are the organizational, structural, and contextual factors that shape what a case can achieve. Outcomes are the documented achievements. The model encodes the relationship between them as production rules: specific condition types generating specific outcome groups through identifiable mechanisms. This is a configurational relationship, not an additive one. Specific combinations of conditions matter more than the total number present.
Outcomes → Sovereignty
Outcome categories are mapped to sovereignty dimensions based on qualitative analysis of what each outcome constructs. For example, DP7: Legal and Rights-Based Protections builds Territorial and Collective Rights sovereignty; IM1: Policy and Regulatory Change builds Territorial sovereignty. When outcomes are achieved, they constitute sovereignty capacity in the corresponding dimensions.
Conditions → Sovereignty (Terrain)
Condition types are mapped to sovereignty dimensions based on the terrain they operate on. For example, Technology Practices and Technology Tools engage Technical and Infrastructure terrain; Governance engages Territorial and Collective Rights terrain. Terrain shows where a case works; capacity shows what it builds. The gap reveals unrealized potential or emergent sovereignty.
Design-Affordance-Sovereignty (D-A-S) Patterns
The dissertation's core theoretical framework posits that design choices create affordances (possibilities for action) that construct sovereignty (collective self-determination capacity). The 17 recursive D-A-S patterns identify recurring configurations where this process operates. Patterns are "recursive" because sovereignty construction feeds back into conditions, enabling further design choices.
Sensitivity Numbers
When condition scores are varied, outcome scores change. The magnitude of change indicates how sensitive outcomes are to that condition. For example, if increasing Governance from 0.50 to 1.00 increases Democratic Practices by +0.080, the sensitivity is 0.08. Larger numbers indicate stronger causal influence.
Validation
The model undergoes multiple validation tests. Internal consistency checks verify that production rules align with empirically documented condition-outcome relationships. Cross-validation tests whether the model's predictions for held-out cases match actual outcomes. Calibration assessment compares predicted outcome distributions against observed distributions.
Condition Interactions
Conditions do not operate independently. Condition interaction analysis tests how pairs of conditions jointly affect outcomes, identifying synergies (combined effect greater than the sum of individual effects), redundancies (combined effect no greater than either individual effect), and dependencies (one condition's effect requiring the other's presence).
Relational Density & Direct Partnerships (M2)
The second model studies relationships because sovereignty construction is not purely internal. Relational density is a composite score (0 to 4) across four dimensions: D1 Partner Diversity (out of 12 types), D2 Relationship Type Range (out of 6), D3 Network Reach (local/national/transnational), D4 Inter-Case Connectivity (out of 4 direct partnerships). Direct partnerships between cases reveal how knowledge and practices flow between initiatives.
Organization Types
The 43 cases are classified into four types: Data Sovereignty Organizations (DSO) focus on Indigenous and community data governance; Digital Cooperatives (DC) organize economic activity through cooperative ownership; Participatory Governance Organizations (PG) embed citizens in public decision-making; and Peer Production Systems (PP) create shared resources through distributed collaboration.

Recursive D-A-S Patterns

17 recursive patterns from the Design-Affordance-Sovereignty framework, organized into three families: sovereignty dimensions (how digital initiatives construct sovereignty in specific domains), sovereignty claimants (who exercises sovereignty claims), and sovereignty debates (the tensions community digital rights projects navigate). Each case either participates in a pattern or does not, based on its condition→mechanism→outcome pathways. Mechanisms constrain which patterns are reachable: structural mechanisms engage political and economic dynamics, enabling mechanisms build technical and community capacity, connective mechanisms navigate rights and flow tensions, protective mechanisms construct legal frameworks, and legitimizing mechanisms mobilize meaning and advocacy.
